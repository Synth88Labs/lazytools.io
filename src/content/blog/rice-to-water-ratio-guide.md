**White long-grain rice uses a 2:1 water-to-rice ratio — 2 cups of water for every 1 cup of rice.** That's the number to remember for everyday rice. But it isn't one-size-fits-all: brown rice wants more water, basmati and jasmine want less, and sushi and wild rice sit at their own points. Get the **rice to water ratio** right for the type in your bag and you'll get fluffy, separate grains instead of a gluey or crunchy pot. Here's every ratio, plus how to use them.

<aside class="key-takeaways">

**Key takeaways**

- **White long-grain:** 2:1 (2 cups water per 1 cup rice) — the default.
- **Per type:** brown 2.5:1, basmati 1.5:1, jasmine 1.5:1, sushi 1.25:1, wild 3:1.
- **It's a ratio,** so water comes out in the same unit as the rice — cups or millilitres both work.
- **Rice roughly triples** — 1 cup dry ≈ 3 cups cooked; plan ½–¾ cup cooked per person.
- **Rinse, then drain well** so you don't add water and skew the ratio.
- **These are absorption amounts** — all the water is soaked up, not boiled off and drained.

## Rice to water ratio by type

These are the standard ratios for the **absorption method**, where the rice soaks up all the water rather than boiling in an excess you drain off. Water is written first, so "2:1" means two parts water to one part rice.

| Rice type | Water : rice | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| White long-grain | 2 : 1 | The everyday default; ~15 min simmer |
| Brown rice | 2.5 : 1 | Bran layer resists water; ~45 min simmer |
| Basmati | 1.5 : 1 | Long aromatic grains stay separate; short simmer |
| Jasmine | 1.5 : 1 | Soft, fragrant Thai rice; short simmer |
| Sushi rice | 1.25 : 1 | Short-grain, meant to be sticky; short simmer |
| Wild rice | 3 : 1 | Not true rice; chewy, needs the most water and time |

Because these are ratios rather than fixed amounts, they scale to any batch size and work in any unit — cups, millilitres, or a rice-cooker cup. Absorption also varies a little with your pan, lid and heat, so treat each ratio as a strong starting point and adjust once you know your kitchen. ## How much water for 1 cup of rice

Multiply your rice amount by the water side of the ratio. A few worked examples:

- **1 cup white long-grain rice → 2 cups water** (2:1).
- **1 cup basmati or jasmine → 1.5 cups water** (1.5:1).
- **2 cups brown rice → 5 cups water** (2.5:1, so 2 × 2.5).
- **1 cup sushi rice → 1.25 cups water** (1.25:1).
- **1 cup wild rice → 3 cups water** (3:1).

The unit only has to be consistent within the recipe. If you measure rice in millilitres, measure water in millilitres too: 200 mL of white rice needs 400 mL of water. Whatever cup or scoop you use for the rice, use the same one for the water.

## How much cooked rice you get

Rice expands dramatically as it absorbs water and its starch swells — roughly **tripling in volume**. As a rule of thumb, **1 cup of dry rice becomes about 3 cups cooked**, and brown and wild rice puff up a touch more.

For portioning, plan about **½ to ¾ cup of cooked rice per person** as a side dish, or more if rice is the centre of the plate. So one cup of dry rice — about three cups cooked — comfortably serves four as a side. That triple-in-volume rule is also why you never want a pan crammed full of dry rice: it needs room to grow. ## The absorption method, step by step

The ratios above assume this method, which is how most stovetop and rice-cooker rice is made.

1. **Rinse the rice** under cold water until the water runs clear, swirling and draining a few times. This washes off surface starch for fluffier, less sticky grains. Drain it well so you don't carry extra water into the pot — the exception is sushi rice, where a little stickiness is wanted.
2. **Measure water by the ratio** for your rice type and add it to the pan.
3. **Bring it to a boil,** uncovered, over medium-high heat.
4. **Cover, drop to low, and simmer** without lifting the lid or stirring. White rice takes around 15 minutes; **brown rice needs about 45 minutes** because its bran resists water (soaking it first shortens this).
5. **Rest off the heat, still covered, for 5–10 minutes.** This lets the moisture redistribute so the bottom isn't wet and the top isn't dry.
6. **Fluff with a fork** to separate the grains, and serve.

## Common mistakes

- **Lifting the lid or stirring while it simmers.** Both release steam and disturb the grains, giving you unevenly cooked, gummy rice. Trust the timer and leave it be.
- **Skipping the rest.** Fluffing the instant the heat goes off gives you a wet bottom and dry top. The 5–10 minute rest fixes that.
- **Not draining after rinsing.** Rinsing is good, but water clinging to wet rice adds to the pot and throws off the ratio. Drain thoroughly.
- **Using white-rice water for brown rice.** Brown rice needs the higher 2.5:1 ratio and a longer simmer; give it a white-rice amount and it comes out crunchy.
- **Applying these ratios to boil-and-drain cooking.** If you cook rice like pasta in a big pot of water you pour off, the water amount doesn't matter — these ratios only apply to the absorption method, where the water is meant to be fully soaked up.

## Stopwatch vs timer

They are opposites, and mixing them up is the most common confusion:

- A **stopwatch counts up** from zero. You start it, the activity happens, you stop it, and it tells you how long that took. It is open-ended.
- A **countdown timer counts down** from a set time to zero and alerts you at the end. It is bounded.

Use a stopwatch to measure something whose length you do not know in advance (how long a task took, a workout, a lap). Use a timer to limit something to a fixed length (a 25-minute focus block, a 3-minute egg). If you want the countdown instead, LazyTools has a [countdown timer](/productivity/countdown-timer/) and a [Pomodoro timer](/productivity/pomodoro-timer/).

## Lap vs split: the real difference

This is the part worth getting right, because the two words are often used loosely. On most stopwatches the convention is:

- **Lap time** = the duration of a single segment, the time since you last pressed the button.
- **Split time** = the cumulative elapsed time from the start to the moment you pressed.

An example makes it clear. Say you run four laps of a track and press Lap at the end of each:

| Lap | Lap time (segment) | Split time (total) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1:02 | 1:02 |
| 2 | 1:03 | 2:05 |
| 3 | 1:01 | 3:06 |
| 4 | 0:58 | 4:04 |

The **lap** column shows how fast each individual lap was, so you can see you sped up at the end (0:58 was your quickest). The **split** column shows your running total, so after three laps you were at 3:06. Notice the relationship: **each lap time is the current split minus the previous split** (2:05 − 1:02 = 1:03). A good stopwatch shows both columns so you never have to do that subtraction yourself, which is exactly what the [Online Stopwatch](/productivity/stopwatch/) does.

## When to use lap times vs split times

- **Lap times** answer "how was each effort?" Use them to compare repetitions: laps of a pool, intervals in a workout, iterations of a task. They reveal whether you are consistent, speeding up or fading.
- **Split times** answer "how am I doing overall?" Use them to track progress against a target: are you on pace to finish in time? Runners famously chase an even split (second half as fast as the first) or a negative split (faster second half).

Most training uses both at once: lap times to judge each piece, split times to judge the whole.

## How to record laps without stopping

The key skill is pressing **Lap** while the clock keeps running. The stopwatch logs that instant and immediately continues, so you time a sequence of efforts in one continuous session without ever stopping and restarting. Start/Stop is for pausing the whole thing (and you can resume, the elapsed time accumulates); Lap is for marking points along the way; Reset clears everything back to zero.

## Why timing precision matters

A stopwatch is only useful if it is accurate. A well-built browser stopwatch is precise to the **centisecond** (hundredths of a second) and, crucially, measures elapsed time from the real start using the browser's **high-resolution clock**, rather than counting ticks that could be delayed. That distinction matters: if a stopwatch simply added up timer ticks, a brief moment of the tab being busy could make it lose time. ## Everyday uses

A stopwatch with laps is handy far beyond sport:

- **Workouts:** time sets and rest, lap each round of a circuit.
- **Running and swimming:** lap each length or lap, read splits to judge pace.
- **Cooking and brewing:** time steps precisely.
- **Work and study:** time how long tasks actually take to plan better (a reality check many people find eye-opening).
- **Games and challenges:** time attempts and compare laps.

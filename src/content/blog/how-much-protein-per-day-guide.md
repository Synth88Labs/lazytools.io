**Daily protein is best set per kilogram of body weight and scaled to your goal: roughly 0.8 g/kg for general health, 1.2–1.6 g/kg if you're active, and 1.6–2.2 g/kg when building muscle or dieting to preserve it.** For a 70 kg person aiming to build muscle, that's about 112–154 grams a day. The right number depends far more on your body weight and training than on a one-size-fits-all figure.

<aside class="key-takeaways">

**Key takeaways**

- **General health:** ~0.8 g per kg of body weight (the RDA minimum).
- **Active / exercising:** 1.2–1.6 g/kg. **Building muscle / strength:** 1.6–2.2 g/kg.
- **Fat loss while keeping muscle:** 1.8–2.4 g/kg (higher protein preserves lean mass in a deficit).
- **Convert pounds to kg** by dividing by 2.2. A 154 lb person is 70 kg.
- **Spread it out:** ~20–40 g across 3–4 meals aids muscle building more than one big serving.
- More isn't better past ~2.2 g/kg for most people — extra mostly adds calories.

## Why protein is set per kilogram

Protein needs scale with body size and how much you train, so sports-nutrition guidance is expressed **per kilogram of body weight** rather than as a flat number of grams. A 55 kg person and a 95 kg person doing the same training need very different totals. The [protein intake calculator](/calc/protein-intake-calculator/) multiplies your weight by the right factor for your goal and gives a gram range.

## The ranges by goal

These reflect widely-cited guidance from the International Society of Sports Nutrition and the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics:

| Goal | Protein (g/kg) | Example at 70 kg |
| --- | --- | --- |
| General health (RDA) | ~0.8 | ~56 g |
| Recreationally active | 1.2–1.6 | ~84–112 g |
| Building muscle / strength | 1.6–2.2 | ~112–154 g |
| Fat loss (preserve muscle) | 1.8–2.4 | ~126–168 g |

**Building muscle** benefits from more protein to support muscle protein synthesis. **Dieting** actually pushes the range up: in a calorie deficit, higher protein helps you hold onto lean muscle while losing fat, and it's the most filling macronutrient, which helps with hunger.

## How to hit your target

- **Anchor meals around a protein source** — eggs, dairy, meat, fish, tofu, tempeh, legumes.
- **Spread it across the day.** Roughly 20–40 g per meal across 3–4 meals supports muscle building better than loading it all into dinner.
- **Use easy wins** — Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, tinned fish, edamame, or a protein shake to close a gap.
- **Track for a week** to calibrate; most people underestimate how little protein they eat until they count.

## Is more always better?

Up to a point. Most people see no additional muscle benefit beyond about **2.2 g/kg**, and very high intakes mainly add calories. For healthy people, high-protein diets are generally safe, but if you have **kidney disease or other medical conditions**, protein needs and limits change — talk to a doctor or dietitian.

*This is general fitness-nutrition guidance, not medical or personalized dietary advice. Needs vary with age, pregnancy, activity and health conditions.*

## FAQ

**How many grams of protein should I eat a day?**
Multiply your body weight in kilograms by 0.8 (general health), 1.2–1.6 (active), or 1.6–2.2 (building muscle). A 70 kg person building muscle targets about 112–154 g. Enter your weight and goal in the [calculator](/calc/protein-intake-calculator/) for your range.

**How do I convert my weight to kilograms?**
Divide pounds by 2.2. So 154 lb ÷ 2.2 = 70 kg. The calculator accepts pounds or kilograms and converts for you.

**Is 1 gram of protein per pound too much?**
About 1 g per pound is ~2.2 g/kg, near the top of the useful range for building muscle. It's fine for many lifters but offers little extra benefit beyond that for most people.

**Does it matter when I eat protein?**
Spreading protein across the day — roughly 20–40 g per meal — supports muscle protein synthesis better than one large serving. Total daily intake still matters most, but distribution helps.

**Can I get too much protein?**
For healthy people, high intakes are generally safe but mostly just add calories past ~2.2 g/kg. If you have kidney or other health conditions, consult a professional, as your needs may differ.

**Do I need more protein to lose weight?**
Yes, relatively — 1.8–2.4 g/kg during a calorie deficit helps preserve muscle and keeps you fuller, so you lose more fat and less lean mass.

**To work out the score you need on your final exam, use: needed = (target − current grade × (1 − final's weight)) ÷ final's weight.** For example, with an 85% current grade, a 90% target, and a final worth 30% of the course, you need (90 − 85 × 0.70) ÷ 0.30 = **101.7%** — just out of reach, which tells you a 90% isn't attainable from the final alone. The formula also reveals when your target is already locked in no matter how the final goes.

<aside class="key-takeaways">

**Key takeaways**

- **Formula:** needed = (target − current × (1 − w)) ÷ w, where **w** is the final's weight as a decimal.
- Your current grade covers **(1 − w)** of the course; the final covers **w**.
- If the answer is **over 100%**, the target isn't reachable from the final alone.
- If it's **0 or negative**, you've already secured the target regardless of the final.
- This answers "what do I need on my final" — different from scoring a test you've already taken.

## The idea behind the formula

Your course grade is a weighted average. If the final is worth **w** of your grade (say 30%, i.e. w = 0.30), then everything else you've done so far — quizzes, homework, midterms — covers the other **(1 − w)** (70%). Your current grade is the average of that already-completed part.

So your final course grade will be:

> course grade = current × (1 − w) + final × w

Set that equal to your **target** and solve for the final score:

> **needed = (target − current × (1 − w)) ÷ w**

That's the exact score you'd need on the final to land precisely on your target. The [final grade calculator](/calc/final-grade-calculator/) does it instantly and tells you whether it's achievable.

## Worked examples

**Reachable target.** Current 88%, target 85%, final worth 40% (w = 0.40):
needed = (85 − 88 × 0.60) ÷ 0.40 = (85 − 52.8) ÷ 0.40 = **80.5%**. Very doable.

**Out of reach.** Current 85%, target 90%, final worth 30%:
needed = (90 − 85 × 0.70) ÷ 0.30 = (90 − 59.5) ÷ 0.30 = **101.7%**. Above 100%, so a 90% overall isn't possible from the final alone — you'd need extra credit or a lower target.

**Already locked in.** Current 95%, target 80%, final worth 25%:
needed = (80 − 95 × 0.75) ÷ 0.25 = (80 − 71.25) ÷ 0.25 = **35%**. You only need 35% on the final to keep an 80% — you've essentially secured it.

## How to read the result

- **Result over 100%** → not achievable on the final by itself. Lower your target to see what *is* reachable.
- **Result 0 or below** → you've already got your target locked, even with a zero on the final.
- **Result between 0 and 100** → that's the minimum score to hit your target; anything higher exceeds it.

Use it in reverse, too: try a few target grades to see which letter grade is realistically still in play, so you know how hard to push.

## A note on "current grade"

Enter your grade on **everything except the final**. The calculator treats the final as the remaining weighted slice of the course. If your syllabus drops your lowest quiz or has other rules, compute your current grade under those rules first, then plug it in.

## FAQ

**How do I calculate what I need on my final?**
Use needed = (target − current × (1 − w)) ÷ w, where w is the final's weight as a decimal. Or enter your current grade, target and the final's weight into the [calculator](/calc/final-grade-calculator/) and it solves it and tells you if it's achievable.

**What if it says I need more than 100%?**
Then your target can't be reached from the final alone — you'd need extra credit or a lower goal. Try entering a lower target to find the best grade that's still attainable.

**What does the "weight" of the final mean?**
It's the percentage of your overall course grade that the final exam counts for, entered as a decimal (a final worth 30% is 0.30). Your other work covers the remaining 70%.

**Does my current grade include the final?**
No — enter your grade on all the work you've done so far, not counting the final. The final is the remaining portion the formula solves for.

**Can I already have passed no matter what?**
Yes. If the formula returns zero or a negative number, your current grade is high enough that even a 0% on the final still meets your target. The calculator flags this.

**Is this the same as a test grade calculator?**
No. A test grade (EZ grader) tells you the percentage for a test you've already taken. This tool answers the forward-looking question: what score do I still need on the final to reach my goal.
